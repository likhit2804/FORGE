## 1. Hackathon Themes

### Track 1: Civic Pulse, Digital Governance & Public Information

This track focuses on making public information easier to access, improving government services, and helping citizens understand government policies and public institutions. It addresses changing laws and policies, misinformation, digital services, and the difficulty businesses face in understanding government rules.

Possible technologies include Generative AI, image and audio analysis, browser automation, and AI agents.

#### Example Problem Areas

1. **Multilingual Policy, Rights & Scheme Explainer** — An AI system that reads government notifications, welfare schemes, and local laws and explains them in simple and regional languages. It should help citizens and small businesses understand rights, check scheme eligibility, and learn how policies affect them. Voice or audio explanations may also be included.

2. **Transparent Civic Grievance & Infrastructure Triage System** — A platform where citizens report problems such as damaged roads or bridges, pollution, and water leaks using photos, videos, or audio. The system assesses severity, routes reports to relevant authorities, and provides a public resolution dashboard.

3. **Autonomous Public Service & Bureaucracy Execution Agent** — An AI agent that helps citizens complete online government services, such as scholarship applications, licence renewals, and subsidy claims. Using browser automation such as Playwright, it navigates websites, fills forms, handles navigation issues, and provides tracking details or receipts.

4. **Real-Time Fact-Verification & Deepfake Audit Engine** — A system that checks online claims, images, videos, and audio for false or AI-generated content. It compares information with trusted sources, detects manipulated media, and helps trace misleading information.

### Track 2: Climate, Resource Infrastructure & Global Supply Resilience

This track focuses on major environmental and resource-related challenges, such as extreme weather, floods, heatwaves, changes in agricultural production, supply chain disruptions, electricity shortages, and declining natural resources. Unpredictable weather and disruptions in the supply of goods can affect farming, cities, water and electricity supplies, and the transportation of essential products.

Solutions in this track can use predictive AI, satellite data, weather forecasting, image analysis, and AI agents to predict potential risks, manage resources more efficiently, improve transportation and delivery, and coordinate emergency responses.

#### Example Problem Areas

1. **Multilingual Agritech & Resource Advisory Assistant** — An AI assistant that uses weather forecasts, local agricultural market prices (mandi prices), and soil information to advise farmers. It could suggest suitable crops, recommend when to plant or harvest, and help protect crops from changing weather through simple and regional-language text or voice.

2. **Predictive Climate & Micro-Location Yield Engine** — An AI system using satellite images from sources such as Copernicus and ISRO, soil sensor data, and historical weather and monsoon records to predict location-specific climate risks. It could identify drought or unexpected-rainfall risks, estimate crop impact, and suggest loss-reduction measures.

3. **Smart Grid & Urban Resource Allocation Controller** — A low-power or IoT system that monitors water supplies, local solar power systems, and urban traffic. During heatwaves or sudden electricity-demand increases, it adjusts resource distribution to help prevent outages and shortages.

4. **Autonomous Emergency Response & Supply Chain Logistics Swarm** — An AI system coordinating essential-supply delivery during floods or major supply disruptions. It monitors weather and vehicle locations, checks regional supplies, identifies safe routes, and coordinates relief orders and deliveries.

## 3. Evaluation Guidelines & Rules

### Scoring Rubric

Projects are evaluated across four pillars:

#### Technical Architecture & Engineering Depth

- Clean, modular code with efficient API integration and low latency.
- Genuine technical implementation beyond a superficial frontend wrapper or basic API template.
- Examples include custom RAG pipelines, geospatial models, browser-automation loops, and complex state graphs.

#### AI Reasoning & Agentic Execution

- Agentic systems are evaluated on planning, multi-step tool calls, error handling, and self-correction.
- AI systems are evaluated on reasoning and retrieval accuracy, hallucination reduction, and reliable source attribution.

#### Problem Relevance, Impact & USP

- Address meaningful civic, governance, climate, or supply-chain challenges.
- Demonstrate clear differentiation from standard solutions, off-the-shelf chatbots, and traditional non-AI software.
- Show innovation and long-term scalability.

#### Live Demonstration & Robustness

- The application must run live during evaluation without crashing.
- It must gracefully handle unexpected inputs, missing data, and unpredictable network states.

### Strict Technical Compliance Rules

- **No dummy data:** Hardcoded JSON, static mock datasets, and fake placeholder numbers are prohibited.
- Applications must use active backends, real-time APIs, live databases, or official open datasets such as IMD, ISRO Bhuvan, or Data.gov.in.
- Open-source libraries, SDKs, and starter boilerplate are permitted.
- Core logic, AI models, and agent workflows must be integrated and built during the hackathon window.
- Every team member must understand the project architecture, data flow, and AI logic.

### Grounds for Immediate Disqualification

Using fake, static, or hardcoded mock data instead of a live backend or real-time API results in immediate disqualification.
